How search engines work

Crawl. Index. Rank.

Every search engine does the same three jobs before anyone sees a result. SEO is the work of making each one easy for your website.

01

Crawl

Search engines send automated crawlers to discover pages by following links and reading sitemaps. If a crawler can't reach a page, that page can't rank.

02

Index

Each page that gets crawled is analyzed and stored: what it's about, who it's for and how it relates to other pages. Pages that are slow, duplicated or thin may be left out.

03

Rank

When someone searches, the engine pulls the most relevant, trustworthy pages from its index and puts them in order. Google weighs hundreds of signals, and some weigh far more than others.

SEO, the foundation

SEO stands on three legs.

SEO improves your visibility on search results pages and grows organic, non-paid traffic. It stands on three legs: technical optimization, content creation and link building. Remove one and the whole thing wobbles. Go ahead, pull a leg out.

Stable. All three legs working together.

The three legs, up close

What each leg actually involves.

Strong on all three, steady in the rankings.

01 Technical optimization

Technical SEO makes sure search engines can find, read and trust every page. It's the leg most people never see, and the one that quietly holds the others up.

Fast load times on phones and desktops
A clean site structure with clear internal links
Crawlable pages, an XML sitemap and a robots.txt file
Secure (HTTPS), mobile-friendly pages
Structured data that labels your business, services and FAQs
Fixing broken links, redirect chains and duplicate pages

02 Content creation

Content is how a search engine learns what you do and who you do it for. Each page should answer one search clearly and completely, in the words your customers actually use.

Keyword research before a single word is written
One focused page for each service and each location
Titles, headings and descriptions that match the search
Answers to the questions customers ask before they call
Updates as your services, prices and markets change
Writing for people first, search engines second

03 Link building

Links from other trusted websites act as votes of confidence. A few links from relevant, respected sites are worth far more than a pile of links from anywhere.

Local directories, chambers of commerce and associations
Industry publications, suppliers and partners
Earned mentions from news, sponsorships and events
No paid link schemes that can trigger penalties

The priority list, up close

Which factors weigh the most.

Heaviest first, then work down.

Not every ranking factor deserves the same attention. We sort the work by how much it can move your rankings, fix the heaviest items first, then keep working down the list. The exact order depends on your site and your market, but it usually looks something like this.

01 Bowling balls: the heavy hitters

These are the factors that decide whether a page can compete at all. If any of them is missing, polishing the small stuff won't make up for it.

Pages that search engines can crawl and index
A page that matches what the searcher actually wants
Helpful, original content that answers the question fully
Links from trusted, relevant websites
A fast, mobile-friendly site
For local businesses, an accurate and active Google Business Profile

02 Middle weights: the steady gains

Once the foundation is solid, these sharpen how search engines understand and rank each page. Each one is a modest lift on its own; together they add up.

Title tags and headings built around the keyword phrase
Internal links that connect related pages
Structured data for your business, services and FAQs
Reviews, and thoughtful responses to them
Fixing broken links and redirect chains
Images that load quickly and have descriptive alt text

03 Ping pong balls: the fine tuning

Small refinements that are worth doing, but only after the heavier work is in place. Starting here is how a lot of SEO budgets get spent without much to show for it.

Meta description wording and length
Small URL changes on pages that already rank
Image file names
Extra speed gains on pages that are already fast

04 Then we do it all again

Search engines update, competitors move and your business changes. So the list gets weighed again on a regular basis, and whatever is now heaviest moves to the top.

Local SEO

Winning the map.

Relevance, distance and prominence.

For a business that serves Lexington, Louisville or anywhere in Kentucky, the map results often matter more than the blue links below them. Google ranks local results on three things: how well a business matches the search, how close it is to the searcher, and how well known and well reviewed it is.

A complete, accurate Google Business Profile: categories, hours, services and photos
The same business name, address and phone number everywhere you are listed
A steady flow of reviews, with thoughtful responses to each one
Location pages with real local content, not copies with the city name swapped
Links and mentions from local organizations and news
Posts and updates that keep your profile active
SEO and AI answers

SEO in the age of AI answers.

Same foundation, new places to show up.

ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity and Claude increasingly answer questions directly instead of listing links. They still lean on the same foundations: pages they can crawl, content that answers a question clearly, and signals that a business is trustworthy.

That makes strong SEO the base that answer engine optimization builds on. Get the fundamentals right and you're in a position to be the answer, not just a result.
